Why is tracking surface change so hard?

Earth's surface is always moving, flooding, growing and melting — but the tools most people use to watch it have blind spots.

1

Clouds and darkness block optical sensors

Cameras on most satellites need sunlight and a clear sky. On average roughly two-thirds of Earth is cloud covered, so floods, storms and eruptions are often hidden exactly when they matter.

2

Centimetre-scale motion is invisible

Slow ground sinking, volcano swelling or fault creep moves only millimetres to centimetres. Photos cannot show it; phase-based radar can.

3

Revisit gaps break the timeline

Change needs a regular, repeatable record. Irregular passes make it hard to tell a flood from a season.

4

Radar data is hard to understand

Complex SLC files, speckle, phase wrapping and HDF5 formats are a real barrier for students, planners and responders.

5

One wavelength does not fit all surfaces

Dense forest, crops, ice and bare soil each respond differently to radar wavelength.

L-band vs S-band

Longer wavelengths penetrate deeper; shorter ones are more sensitive to small features like leaves and surface roughness.

PropertyL-band (NASA)S-band (ISRO)

Radar glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms you will meet when working with NISAR data.

TermMeaning
SARSynthetic Aperture Radar: moving radar that combines many pulses to act like a very large antenna and give fine resolution.
BackscatterThe fraction of radar energy that returns to the antenna. Bright = strong return, dark = weak return.
SpeckleGrainy noise from random interference of scatterers inside one pixel.
MultilookingAveraging neighbouring pixels to reduce speckle at the cost of resolution.
InSARInterferometric SAR: comparing the phase of two images to measure distance change.
FringeOne full colour cycle in an interferogram, equal to half a wavelength of line-of-sight motion.
CoherenceA 0 to 1 measure of how similar two radar images are. Low coherence means unreliable phase.
Phase unwrappingTurning repeating 0 to 360 degree phase into continuous motion values.
LOSLine of sight: the direction from the ground to the satellite. InSAR only measures motion along it.
PolarizationOrientation of the radar wave, for example HH or HV. Different polarizations highlight different surfaces.
GeocodingPlacing radar pixels onto map coordinates so they overlay with other data.
SweepSARNISAR scanning technique that widens the swath without losing resolution.
Offset trackingFollowing surface texture between two images to measure large motion such as glacier flow.
Dielectric constantHow strongly a material interacts with radar. Wet soil has a high value and returns more energy.

Frequently asked questions

Is the explorer using real NISAR data?

No. The scenes are simulations built from NISAR radar physics so the app runs offline. The Data tab explains how to swap in real granules from ASF Vertex or Earthdata Search.

Why does water look dark in radar?

Calm water reflects the radar beam away from the satellite like a mirror, so very little energy returns. Rough land and buildings scatter energy back and look brighter.

What is an interferogram?

It compares the phase of two radar images taken on different dates. A change in distance between satellite and ground shifts the phase, producing colour fringes.

What is speckle?

Grainy noise created when many scatterers inside one pixel add up randomly. Averaging (multilooking) reduces it.

Why two radar bands?

L-band penetrates vegetation and stays coherent; S-band is more sensitive to leaves, crops and soil surface. Together they describe both structure and surface.

How often does NISAR see the same place?

Every 12 days on an exact-repeat orbit, with ascending and descending passes improving revisit.

    Technology Stack

    What powers the prototype today—and what to use when connecting it to authentic NISAR products.

    🧱

    Frontend

    HTML5 + CSS3 + Vanilla JavaScript
    Responsive layout, navigation, controls, canvas rendering and client-side simulation. No build step or framework required.

    🎨

    Visualization

    Canvas 2D + custom JS
    Procedural radar-like scenes, animated time slider, band comparisons and fringe visualizations. These are illustrative simulations, not measured pixels.

    🛰️

    Mission data layer

    NISAR L1/L2/L3 products (future/live integration)
    RSLC, GUNW, GCOV, GOFF and SME2; data access via ASF DAAC/Vertex and ISRO Bhoonidhi.

    🐍

    Scientific processing

    Python: NumPy, xarray, h5py, rasterio
    Read arrays/HDF5, raster operations, analysis and map preparation. ISCE3 for SAR/InSAR processing; MintPy for time-series deformation.

    🗺️

    GIS & mapping

    QGIS + GeoTIFF / geocoded layers
    Inspect, style and overlay NISAR outputs with boundaries, roads, elevation and exposure layers.

    ☁️

    Data delivery (planned)

    ASF cloud access / Earthdata APIs
    Catalog search, granule selection, authenticated download or cloud workflows. Backend/API integration is not currently implemented in this standalone HTML prototype.

    Architecture

    Browser UI → scenario controls → simulation renderer (current) / NISAR granule loader (planned) → scientific processing (ISCE3/Python) → geocoded layers → map & metrics.

    More Challenges → Practical Solutions

    Risks that matter when turning radar observations into decisions. Radar is powerful, but it is not a magic answer.

    ChallengeWhy it mattersSolution / mitigationProduct or method
    Layover & shadowSteep terrain distorts or hides slopes in side-looking SAR.Use terrain correction, DEM-assisted interpretation and complementary ascending/descending views; flag shadow/layover.RTC, DEM, geometry masks
    Temporal / spatial decorrelationVegetation, snow or rapid change can destroy interferometric phase reliability.Use coherence masks, shorter temporal baselines where available, multi-date analysis; switch to amplitude/offset tracking for large motion.RSLC, RIFG/GUNW, GOFF
    Atmospheric delayWater vapour can mimic broad deformation signals.Compare multiple interferograms, inspect spatial patterns, use weather/context data and time-series methods; report uncertainty.InSAR time series / MintPy
    Flood ambiguityDark returns can also mean smooth surfaces; urban double-bounce may be bright.Combine pre/post SAR, thresholding, terrain/land-cover context and field/optical validation; distinguish permanent water from new inundation.GCOV / RTC + change map
    Vegetation & soil moisture confoundingBackscatter varies with structure, moisture, incidence and season.Use calibrated multi-date comparisons, dual-band/polarization/context and avoid treating brightness as a direct biomass or moisture measurement.GCOV, polarimetry, SME2
    Geolocation & alignmentMisregistered layers create false change boundaries.Check CRS, geocoding, DEM and co-registration; use consistent grids and quality flags.GSLC/GCOV + GIS QA
    Data volume & latencyLarge HDF5 granules and processing can delay local workflows.Filter by AOI/date/product, use cloud or chunked reads, cache derived tiles and show processing status.ASF search + Python/cloud
    Uncertainty & false alarmsSimulated or noisy maps may be mistaken for definitive alerts.Display confidence/quality layers, thresholds, source date and validation status; require human review for response decisions.QA masks + validation
